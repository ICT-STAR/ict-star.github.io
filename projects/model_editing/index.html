STAR GROUP   /   MODEL EDITING

Editing knowledge.
Preserving intelligence.

How do we change what a language model knows
without breaking what it can do?

A research thread by Wanli Yang, Fei Sun & collaborators
Four papers · 2024–2026

Model editing promises to update a language model’s knowledge without retraining it from scratch. But changing a fact is only part of the problem: the updated model must still reason, generate coherent language, and use its new knowledge beyond the edit prompt. Our research asks what it takes to make those updates reliable.

Across four studies, we move from discovering collateral damage to explaining a failure mechanism, then from measuring real-world effectiveness to building a more scalable editor. Together, these works connect three requirements: understand the update, evaluate it honestly, and preserve the model’s broader abilities.

01   /   FINDINGS OF ACL 2024

The Butterfly Effect
of Model Editing

One factual update can disrupt a whole model.

We show that even a single ROME edit can severely damage general capabilities, while challenging edit sequences expose collapse across multiple methods. Perplexity offers an efficient signal of downstream degradation; our HardEdit dataset helps stress-test these failures. The lesson: editing success alone cannot tell us whether a model remains useful.

The Butterfly Effect of Model Editing: Few Edits Can Trigger Large Language Models Collapse

02   /   FINDINGS OF EMNLP 2024

The Fall of ROME

From observing collapse to understanding why.

We trace a class of ROME failures to inconsistent use of contextualized and unprefixed key representations. For subjects at the first token, this mismatch can shrink the update’s denominator and amplify the parameter change. Using prefixed keys consistently stabilizes the update; matching the context at evaluation recovers edit efficacy. This explains a concrete single-edit failure, while cumulative editing remains a broader challenge.

The Fall of ROME: Understanding the Collapse of LLMs in Model Editing

03   /   ACL 2025

The Mirage
of Model Editing

Does the model know it, or does the test help it?

We introduce QAEdit and WILD to evaluate edits through freely generated answers. Teacher forcing and answer-length hints can make synthetic evaluations overly optimistic. On Llama-2-7b-chat and QAEdit, mean single-edit reliability across six editors drops from 96.8% to 38.5% under WILD. Realistic evaluation turns apparent success into a more demanding test of usable knowledge.

The Mirage of Model Editing: Revisiting Evaluation in the Wild

04   /   ICLR 2026

Fine-tuning Done Right
in Model Editing

Revisit the procedure. Recover the potential.

Fine-tuning’s weakness in editing is partly a training-procedure problem: optimizing each edit to convergence can overfit and interfere with other edits. LocFT-BF combines epoch-based mini-batches with carefully localized parameter updates. Tested with WILD, it scales to 100K edits and 72B-parameter models while largely retaining general capabilities. Reliability, paraphrase generalization, and capability preservation remain distinct measures of success.
